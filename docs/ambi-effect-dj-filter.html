Ambi Effect DJ Filter

s3g Ambi Effect DJ Filter 64 is the first member of the Ambi Effect family: insert processing for complete ACN/SN3D fields from first through seventh order. Its global control moves continuously from low-pass filtering on the left, through an open center, to high-pass filtering on the right. Twelve persistent pickup trims can vary that position around the body.

The effect borrows the directional body learned through Listener Mode, but uses that body as an audio-processing topology. It is a separate architecture from the fixed third-order, 24-point 3OAFX Effects.

Signal Flow

1OA-7OA ACN/SN3D field
-> unchanged encoded field --------------------------+
|                                                     |
+-> decode Tetra 4 / Cube 8 / Icosa 12 pickups       |
    -> global DJ position + per-pickup filter trims   |
    -> Local / Cross / Diffuse / Roaming topology    |
    -> subtract the original pickup signals           |
    -> optional directional wet mask                  |
    -> re-encode only that residual ------------------+
                                                      |
                         dry field + residual -> MIX -> OUT
  1. The selected active-order field is copied to the dry path and decoded at each virtual pickup in the chosen auditory body.
  2. Two continuously running filter wings form low-pass and high-pass versions of every pickup signal.
  3. The global DJ control and each pickup's bipolar trim determine an independent low-pass/open/high-pass position; the selected topology then relates those filtered signals across the body.
  4. The original pickup signals are subtracted, leaving only the change made inside the auditory body.
  5. The optional directional mask scales that residual at each pickup, allowing one region to stay wet while other directions approach the untouched field.
  6. A regularized encoder returns the masked residual to the active-order field, where MIX scales it against the unchanged input.
  7. OUT applies final gain and channels above the selected order remain silent.

Re-encoding only the processed-minus-original residual keeps material outside the selected pickup subspace intact. With every pickup trim at zero, the exact center makes filter depth and topology depth zero, so the residual disappears and the active-order field passes unchanged apart from OUT. A nonzero pickup trim deliberately keeps that point active while the global control is centered. Both filter wings continue running internally, which keeps automation across the center smooth.

Order and Auditory Body

OrderActive channelsAUTO body
1OA4TETRA 4
2OA9CUBE 8
3OA-7OA16-64ICOSA 12

AUTO follows the mapping above. You can override it with TETRA 4, CUBE 8, or ICOSA 12. Choosing a smaller body at a higher order gives fewer, broader processing anchors and leaves more of the encoded field outside the correction subspace. The plugin always exposes 64 input and output pins; channels above the selected order are inactive.

Controls

ControlFunction
ORDERSelects 1OA through 7OA and therefore 4 through 64 active field channels.
BODYSelects AUTO, TETRA 4, CUBE 8, or ICOSA 12.
DJ FILTERSweeps from maximum low-pass at the left through OPEN at center to maximum high-pass at the right.

Pickup Editing and Meters

Click a numbered point in the auditory-body display to select it, then drag the large bipolar trim rail below the body. Double-click either the point or the rail to return that pickup to zero trim. The twelve pickup parameters are independently automatable, even when the current body activates only four or eight of them.

Each point contains a pre-effect input meter. Its fill rises from the bottom over a -60 dB display range, so it shows which virtual pickups are actually receiving the incoming encoded field before filtering, topology, or masking. The small marker below each point shows its LP/HP trim. The point outline represents its current wet-mask gain: bright approaches fully wet and dim approaches the untouched field.

Directional Wet Mask

The mask is applied after filtering and topology but before residual re-encoding. MASK: OFF preserves the normal all-wet behavior. Raising MASK makes the region nearest AZ/EL retain more of the effect while other pickups contribute less correction. Since the dry path remains the original HOA field, a masked-out direction does not pass through a replacement decoder.

The dashed aim circle in the body display shows the mask center and approximate width. The mask follows the currently selected Tetra, Cube, or Icosa body, and its pickup gains are smoothed during automation.

Topology Modes

  • LOCAL returns each filtered pickup to its own directional anchor.
  • CROSS sends each filtered pickup to the opposing anchor.
  • DIFFUSE replaces each pickup with the average of its nearest neighbors on the selected body.
  • ROAMING continuously moves pickup signals around a connected path over the body. ROAM RATE controls one complete traversal.

Topology is deliberately coupled to each pickup's filter depth. A pickup at its effective OPEN position receives no topology correction; with every trim at zero, the global center remains a reliable neutral point during one-knob performance sweeps.

Workflow

  1. Place the effect after an ambisonic encoder or encoded-media source and before the decoder.
  2. Match ORDER to the incoming field. Start with BODY: AUTO, TOPOLOGY: LOCAL, and the DJ filter at OPEN.
  3. Sweep left to remove high frequencies or right to remove low frequencies. Add resonance after establishing the useful range.
  4. Select active pickup points and offset a few toward LP or HP. Their meters make it easier to place stronger variation where the encoded field is actually active.
  5. Try CROSS or DIFFUSE with a moderate amount for spatial redistribution, then use ROAMING when the filtering should travel through the field.
  6. Raise MASK, aim its azimuth/elevation, and adjust width when the effect should occupy one directional region while the rest remains progressively dry.
  7. At high orders, compare ICOSA 12 with a deliberately smaller Cube or Tetra body. The smaller selection is not lower-order decoding; it is a coarser set of processing anchors inside the selected order.
