Processor Fault

s3g Processor Fault is a zero-audio-input, eight-output CLAP instrument that turns a generated byte field, an arbitrary file, or the waveform ancestry of a PCM WAV into continuously changing audio. It combines raw-data scanning, stochastic breakpoint geometry, damaged codec models, receiver-driven bass reconstruction, protocol modulation, and a bounded pressure, wavefold, and resonant-feedback stage. It can run continuously or respond as a velocity-sensitive MIDI instrument.

Raw-data scanning reads bytes as a moving waveform, while stochastic breakpoint geometry connects selected values with evolving curves. The codec section then introduces prediction errors, quantization, missing blocks, or damaged reconstruction as sound-generating processes rather than as file-format emulation.

The native editor uses a compact 1356-by-720 layout with three upper pages. SOUND keeps the field, codec, a large FM-style algorithm chart, concise M1/M2/M3 route summaries, and the immediate BASS CORE amount together for fast patch decisions. BASS LAB exposes the receiver, pitch, body, articulation, and low-width controls. MOD LAB gives each operator a complete card without making the primary page wider or taller. An always-visible MOD ON/MOD OFF button bypasses the three-operator graph from any page; the independent bass core remains available. Patch controls, performance state, and the envelope editor remain in one shared lower strip. Hosts may reduce the responsive viewport and use its scroll access without rescaling control hit areas.

Workflow

  1. Set a REAPER track to eight channels and insert s3g Processor Fault.
  2. Choose a preset or begin with INIT. Use OPEN ANY to replace the generated field with any file. A recognized WAV opened from INIT selects WAVE TRACE.
  3. Use FREE for continuous sound, or select MIDI and play notes from the DAW.
  4. Set SCAN, GEOMETRY, CHAOS, and FOLD for the underlying motion.
  5. Choose a codec, then use RATE, BITS, CARRIER, and DAMAGE to break its continuity.
  6. Raise BASS CORE, then open BASS LAB to choose how the codec becomes low-frequency excitation. Start with DIVIDE for stable subharmonics, DEMOD for contour, or ERROR for percussive faults; use TRACE to retain the raw codec identity.
  7. On SOUND, choose a CURATED CIRCUIT and read its operator flow in the chart. Start with a factory patch or CURATED RANDOM for a bounded variation, then open MOD LAB when you want to change an algorithm or an operator's source, destination, rate, ratio, index, feedback, clock, or envelope response.
  8. Raise DRIVE, SHRED, and RESONANCE to expose the byte-field contours and reinforce the modal body.
  9. Choose a ROUTE and set SPREAD for the eight-channel distribution.

A useful first experiment is a low displayed codec update rate with high FOLD, followed by enough DRIVE and SHRED to turn the held vectors into pronounced curves. Then raise BASS CORE, select DIVIDE, and lower TRACE until the codec's carrier crossings resolve into a low body without disappearing completely.

Performance

FREE preserves Fault's continuous generator behavior and bypasses the amplitude envelope. MIDI makes the same field engine a monophonic, last-note-priority voice. Note-on retriggers the attack from the current envelope level, velocity controls voice level, and releasing the active note returns to the most recently held note. When no notes remain, the release runs to silence and the field freezes once the envelope becomes idle.

MIDI note 60 (C4) is the patch's reference note. KEY TRACK determines which domains receive its equal-tempered ratio: SCAN preserves the original behavior by changing traversal and breakpoint timing while leaving the bass target fixed; BODY keys the divider and modal-filter target while the field keeps its free-running scan rate; and BODY + SCAN keys both. OCTAVE places that target at C2, C3, or C4 for -2 OCT, -1 OCT, or UNISON (zero-octave offset), and GLIDE smooths changes between held notes. Codec crossings can pull the measured result away from an exact oscillator pitch, especially with loaded material; this coupling is what keeps the low voice part of Fault rather than turning it into a conventional synth layer.

A MIDI note-on also excites EXCITE, using velocity to accent the codec-derived attack without bending the body pitch. In FREE mode the receiver remains continuously driven by codec crossings, envelopes, and damage rather than retriggering a separate drum voice.

The envelope graph is directly editable. Drag the attack and release endpoints horizontally, the decay breakpoint in both axes, and the sustain breakpoint vertically. The dashed live-level trace and highlighted breakpoint follow the active MIDI envelope stage.

PLAY and STOP remain global: they fade and freeze either mode. Presets, CURATED RANDOM, and MUTATE shape the timbre but leave the selected performance mode and ADSR settings alone. In MIDI mode, any modulation operator set to ADSR also follows this envelope at its index, so attacks can assemble a modulation stack and releases can withdraw it without changing the operator's stored depth. FIXED leaves that operator running at full index. In FREE mode both settings behave as fully open.

Byte Field and Geometry

The default source is a deterministic 65,536-byte virtual field. GEN FIELD captures the currently selected CODEC as a hidden generation profile, rolls a new seed, and builds data chosen to expose that codec's characteristic behavior. The source readout shows the captured profile in brackets. Changing CODEC afterward does not rewrite the field, so the same material can be compared across codecs; press GEN FIELD again to tailor a new field to the new selection.

The profiles use different data grammars rather than post-processing the same noise: differential and predictive modes receive continuous slopes with controlled innovations; companders receive quiet detail and sparse peaks; speech-like modes receive voiced, residual, and excitation structure; DISC receives repeated blocks and markers; SUBBAND receives separated slow and alternating detail; TRANSFORM receives sparse short-block spectra; MODEM receives symbol plateaus; FAX receives repeated scanlines; SIGMA 1-BIT receives slowly changing oversampled curves; APT and HF FAX receive sync, phasing, telemetry, and image regions; HELL receives doubled raster glyphs; MORSE and SPARK CW receive dot-dash keying fields; BAUDOT RTTY receives start/data/stop frames; and SSTV receives sync, porch, separator, and color scanlines. For an arbitrary file, OPEN ANY reads from byte zero and maps every byte to an unsigned 8-bit bipolar sample.

With Decode WAVE data into eight lanes enabled in the open dialog, Fault parses RIFF chunks, jumps to the actual data chunk, and decodes integer PCM at 8, 16, 24, or 32 bits or floating-point PCM at 32 or 64 bits. It applies bounded gain recovery, remaps every source frame into eight related lanes, and requantizes those lanes into the byte field. Mono becomes eight short-offset traces; stereo becomes four related pairs; sources with eight or more channels retain eight native lanes. This preserves contour, rhythm, and some pitch identity without retaining a recoverable clean signal. Disable the option to include the WAV header and all other bytes in the older literal-byte treatment.

Raw files and the prepared eight-lane field are limited to 64 MiB. Longer sources show PARTIAL in the source readout. Internally, a shorter or irregularly sized source repeats far enough to form a power-of-two scan ring. The codec BITS control requantizes the field again later in the chain.

Codec Damage

The codec section treats the generated or file-derived signal as material for destructive encoding. These modes are synthesis models, not interoperable media codecs. Their design sources are collected in the Codec references.

  • PCM applies direct bit quantization.
  • DELTA quantizes the difference from a running predictor.
  • ADPCM quantizes prediction error with a multiplicatively adapted step: large codes expand it quickly and small codes contract it slowly.
  • MU-LAW and A-LAW encode and decode eight-bit G.711-style sign, segment, and mantissa codewords. BITS removes low codeword planes below eight bits, while DAMAGE flips code bits.
  • CELP uses damaged pitch, gain, and codebook behavior. Missing packets replay pitch-synchronous history with progressive attenuation, followed by a short recovery crossfade. It is not a G.729 encoder, decoder, or bitstream implementation.
  • DISC introduces block errors whose duration selects the response: short gaps are interpolated with a 32-sample lookahead, medium gaps repeat or extrapolate fading history, and long gaps decay toward mute. It does not implement CD or DVD framing, modulation, or error correction.
  • CVSD tracks the signal with a one-bit adaptive slope. Three identical decisions expand the step; alternating decisions contract it; bit errors and a clock-relative reconstruction filter expose failure.
  • SUBBAND uses a downsampled two-band Haar quadrature-mirror bank with independently damaged adaptive quantizers for its low and high bands.
  • LPC replaces increasing amounts of prediction residual with pulse and noise excitation through an adaptive all-pole model.
  • TRANSFORM performs 32-sample DCT blocks with a damaged scale factor, frequency-weighted coefficient bit allocation, missing blocks, and coefficient-bit upsets.
  • PREDICT uses a second-order predictor with quantized residuals, missing corrections, and occasional residual upsets.
  • MODEM maps amplitude into damaged FSK-like symbols and carrier tones.
  • FAX maps level and slope into fixed QPSK, eight-QAM, or sixteen-QAM constellations, then exposes raised-cosine symbol transitions, training sequences, equalizer lag, and phase slips.
  • SIGMA 1-BIT uses a first-order integrated error-feedback loop followed by a bit-clock-relative reconstruction filter.
  • HYBRID combines a predictive base layer with a correction layer that can thin, vanish, or invert.
  • APT treats the signal as weather-satellite image brightness and amplitude-modulates a 2400 Hz subcarrier. Its 2080-word composite line carries paired image regions, telemetry wedges, minute markers, a seven-cycle 1040 Hz sync train, and a seven-pulse 832 Hz sync train. DAMAGE adds word errors, stale video, line slips, fading, carrier phase jumps, and receiver noise. It models the Automatic Picture Transmission sound and line grammar, not an interoperable RF transmission.
  • HF FAX scans brightness as frequency between the historical 1500 Hz black and 2300 Hz white limits around 1900 Hz. Each line begins with phasing and start bars; DAMAGE introduces wrong pixels, black dropouts, line slant, flutter, and phase slips.
  • HELL is a Feld-Hellschreiber-style 5-by-7 raster teleprinter with doubled vertical pixels and keyed 900 Hz ink. Clock mismatch and DAMAGE smear, repeat, drop, or invert raster cells.
  • MORSE converts the field into International Morse letters, preserving one-unit dots and intra-element gaps, three-unit dashes and character gaps, and a keyed 700 Hz tone. DAMAGE drops elements and adds chirp and key-edge roughness.
  • SPARK CW uses the same Morse timing but replaces the clean continuous-wave tone with damped, chirping, harmonic, noise-rich spark bursts.
  • BAUDOT RTTY sends ITA2-style five-bit letters with a start bit and stop interval as 170 Hz-shift FSK around 1700 Hz. It includes letters/figures shifts; DAMAGE flips data and shift decisions.
  • SSTV uses a slow-scan-television-style line: 1200 Hz sync, 1500 Hz porch and separators, then three 320-pixel color scans mapped from 1500 to 2300 Hz. DAMAGE causes false sync, frequency errors, and horizontal line slips.

For APT, 4160 Hz gives the historical half-second composite line. Moving the control right lowers the displayed rate, reaching below 3 Hz at 48 kHz.

In older operator-only patches, the same control places the BODY/RING modal base from approximately 30 to 110 Hz. Raising the dedicated BASS CORE progressively transfers that modal root to the BASS LAB pitch controls.
DAMAGERaises mode-specific prediction error, packet loss, stale blocks, coefficient removal, bit errors, phase slips, or correction-layer failure.

Bass Core

BASS LAB reconstructs a coherent low voice from the selected codec rather than placing an unrelated sub oscillator underneath it. Codec and modulation crossings clock a rounded binary divider; the selected receiver weights that divided signal with the waveform, transmission envelope, or codec errors; and a moderately damped modal filter focuses the result around the selected pitch. Because the low cycles must come from the running codec, their timing and grain retain the patch's transmission character. TRACE determines how much of the raw Fault path remains audible. The compact BASS CORE control on SOUND is the same BODY amount shown in the lab.

ControlMeaning
RECEIVERSelects how codec output weights the divided crossings. DIRECT follows waveform activity, DEMOD follows the transmission envelope, DIVIDE exposes the rounded power-of-two division most directly, and ERROR gates it with dropped frames and large discontinuities.
BODY / BASS CORERaises the receiver-derived low body and progressively transfers the modal root from the older carrier-related placement to the dedicated pitch target. Zero preserves the previous Fault path.
EXCITESets the depth and decay of a pitch-neutral emphasis on receiver excitation. MIDI velocity triggers the accent; it does not add a sine layer or a falling-pitch kick contour.
TRACERetains the raw codec identity above the reconstructed body. At 100 percent the signal follows the previous Fault path exactly; lower values reveal more of the receiver-driven core in proportion to BODY.
KEY TRACK / PITCH TRACKINGSCAN sends MIDI pitch to field traversal only. BODY keys the low divider/filter target while scan remains free. BODY + SCAN applies the note ratio to both domains.
OCTAVEPlaces the C4 reference target at -2 OCT (C2), -1 OCT (C3), or UNISON / 0 offset (C4) for the bass body.
GLIDESmooths movement between body pitches, from a near-immediate transition to a long portamento.
LOW WIDTHControls only the spatial residual below roughly twice the body target. Zero centers the low band across the eight lanes; 100 percent retains its full lane-to-lane width while leaving the higher codec detail spatial.

The bass core and the modulation graph can reinforce one another, but they have separate enable paths. M1, M2, and M3 may still address BODY, RING, or STRIKE to animate the same resonant region, while their codec, clock, and data changes alter the crossings that generate the subharmonic. MOD OFF removes those operator contributions without switching off the receiver, dedicated pitch, excite amount, trace blend, or low-width processing.

Protocol Modulation

Protocol modulation adds three operators, M1, M2, and M3, and six selectable stacking algorithms to the codec. It is a distant cousin of FM synthesis: one process acts on another, but an operator is not limited to a sinusoid and its destination is not limited to oscillator frequency. Raster sync, telegraph keying, framed bits, image scanlines, field data, and the codec's own output can become control signals. This makes the historically functional parts of a transmission system available as timbre structure without exposing a large modulation matrix.

All three operators share the same source vocabulary. The tonal sources are SINE, TRIANGLE, and stepped NOISE. FIELD follows the current byte-field signal; SYNC and GATE supply narrow and square pulses. APT, HF FAX, HELL, MORSE, SPARK CW, BAUDOT RTTY, and SSTV reuse simplified line, raster, keying, framing, and scan grammars as modulation shapes. FEEDBACK derives a bounded operator from the codec's recent output, turning the carrier/modulator distinction into a recursive loop.

The SOUND page draws the selected topology as an FM-style node chart: arrows show inter-operator influence, BUS or MUX nodes show parallel and time-slotted combinations, and the final node identifies the independent destinations. The three text summaries beneath it retain each operator's source, destination, and envelope mode. Its CURATED CIRCUIT menu installs one of two musically bounded operator recipes for the chosen topology while preserving the rest of the patch. MOD LAB repeats the live chart beside the full operator cards; its ALGORITHM menu changes only the graph, leaving the deep settings under direct control.

Protocol sources are structural appropriations, not interoperable encoders. Their sync regions and alphabets are intentionally treated as compositional objects. The curated layer favors BODY, RING, STRIKE, FOLD, and SCAN, so telegraph and image grammars become pitch, decay, impact, shape, or motion instead of being copied directly into the audio stream. The older carrier, deviation, data, and damage paths remain available when deliberate broadband fracture is wanted.

Integrated Nonlinear Stage

The shaping stage follows the codec so held steps and prediction errors become wave geometry rather than remaining quiet digital artifacts.

  • DRIVE raises level into pressure and saturation.
  • SHRED moves the nonlinear path toward stronger folding and edge generation.
  • RESONANCE raises a bounded internal feedback loop. Low settings follow a smoothed spectral-center estimate; above roughly one third, the loop progressively settles into a darker fixed body. BODY modulation can pull that body down by another two octaves, while the dedicated modal voice guarantees a coherent bass result even when the selected codec has a strong radio-frequency carrier.
  • OUTPUT sets the final trim after internal calibration.

Feed-forward calibration and a slow, bounded level correction reduce large loudness jumps among codec and shaping settings. They do not normalize away transient or spectral differences. The final path removes DC and limits output to finite sample bounds.

Eight-Channel Routing

ROUTE changes how each output lane addresses the byte field:

  • PARALLEL keeps lanes near a shared read path.
  • DEINTERLEAVE distributes adjacent field data across eight lane-strided reads. For a prepared WAV, it follows the eight derived waveform lanes directly.
  • PLANES offsets lanes into separate field regions.
  • SHUFFLED uses repeatable hashed read addresses.
  • DIVERGENT gives lanes different strides and long-term trajectories.

SPREAD operates on the mean plus each channel's residual. At zero, all lanes collapse toward the instantaneous eight-channel mean. At one, their full differences remain. Bounded energy compensation prevents spread changes from acting mainly as gain changes.

The display shows eight recent output waveforms. Each trace scales itself to reveal shape and is therefore not an absolute level meter; use the peak readout or a following Multichannel Meter for level comparison.

Presets and Patch Actions

The factory bank treats both the modulation graph and bass reconstruction path as parts of the patch rather than optional afterthoughts. INIT remains neutral with M1, M2, and M3 off and BASS CORE at zero. The twelve active presets demonstrate every algorithm exactly twice and store all three operators' settings together with receiver, body, excite, trace, key tracking, glide, octave, and low width. Their M2 and M3 paths follow the ADSR; M1 follows it where a crossed or transcoding graph benefits from the complete stack entering and leaving together. Editing a sound control changes the menu state to CUSTOM.

  • PLAY resumes the field from its frozen position with a 20 ms fade-in.
  • STOP fades to silence over 20 ms, then freezes the synthesis engine without resetting it.
  • OPEN ANY loads any file as a byte field. Its enabled WAV option skips the container header and prepares eight lossy waveform-derived lanes; disabling the option uses every file byte literally.
  • GEN FIELD leaves file mode, captures the selected codec as the field profile, and creates new codec-oriented source data while retaining the remaining control settings.
  • CURATED RANDOM chooses one of the twelve proven modulation and bass circuits, retains its sub or percussion identity, then varies field motion, codec settings, receiver, body, excite, trace, glide, low width, operator timing, ratios, indices, feedback, and nonlinear controls inside conservative limits. It does not invent an arbitrary source/destination graph. The guardrails favor BODY or BODY + SCAN key tracking, the lower octave settings, a centered low band, moderate texture and chaos, low codec damage and shred, bounded target-specific indices, and useful resonance; they exclude deviation, data, and damage from generated primary routes.
  • MUTATE makes smaller changes around the current patch and usually preserves its seed, codec family, receiver, and octave. It normally keeps the current circuit; occasional circuit, receiver, or octave changes stay inside the same bass and destination guardrails. Repeated mutation therefore cannot gradually walk a curated patch back to every destructive endpoint.
  • UNDO restores the state before the most recent preset, source, field, randomize, or mutate action.

Preset, source, field, codec, bit-depth, and routing changes crossfade instead of resetting abruptly. The automatable Run parameter mirrors the PLAY and STOP buttons. REAPER project state stores the transport state, performance mode, ADSR settings, complete operator graph, complete bass reconstruction setup, field seed, captured generation profile, and external source path; held notes and source bytes are not embedded. Older states recall with the bass core neutral so their previous sound is preserved. If the file is unavailable on recall, Fault falls back to the generated field and shows the missing source in its header.
