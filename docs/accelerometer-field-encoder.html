Ambi Encoder Modal

Ambi Encoder Modal is a gong-focused modal instrument that publishes first-, second-, or third-order ACN/SN3D directly. Its four body families are GONG AGENG, BELL BRONZE, the large Korean JING, and the small Korean KKWAENGGWARI. Eight virtual radiation points hear one shared 24-mode body and turn its changing vibration pattern into a spatial field.

The instrument is designed for immediate results: select a preset, choose a gong body and exciter, then shape Coupling, Energy, and projection. RANDOM starts from a curated gong state and makes bounded variations; it cannot select the unrelated plant, paper, shell, glass, wire, or membrane models retained internally only for migration of older sessions.

Signal Flow

  1. Ambient force, double mallets, a mallet roll, scrape/rub, bowed rim, strike, the mono Gong Force In, or a MIDI note excites the body.
  2. Twenty-four damped modes form close pairs and spectral clusters. Strike position changes which modal lobes receive energy.
  3. COUPLING introduces shared slow and fast amplitude/frequency motion; ENERGY adds bounded impact-dependent pitch relaxation and delayed upper-mode buildup.
  4. Eight radiation points sample different modal positions and angles. Contact Detail and Air Radiation interpolate between close structural vibration and radiated gong sound.
  5. Projection assigns those observations to a rotatable angular field and encodes the result as ACN/SN3D.
  6. The optional Listener analyzes the internal field and can influence later gestures without returning audio to the resonator.

Gong Bodies

BodyModal emphasis
Gong AgengLow rumble and upper ding clusters around a 44.5 Hz reference landmark, with long differentiated envelopes and strong coupled evolution.
Bell BronzeHum, prime, tierce, quint, and nominal-like regions with close non-axisymmetric pairs and a poised bell warble.
JingA large Korean gong family referenced to the measured 114 Hz resting fundamental of a 382 mm instrument. Exact low harmonics soften below pitch under force and recover upward, moving against fixed short-lived and late beat components.
KkwaenggwariA compact Korean hand-gong family with reduced low-frequency radiation and strong attack/radiation regions around 1, 2, and 4 kHz. Its broad inharmonic pairs and brighter contact pulse favor rhythmic playing.

These are research-informed, playable archetypes rather than identifications of one specimen. Sangjin Cho’s jing analysis motivates the model’s emphasis on drive position, blow strength, beating, and frequency softening; published kkwaenggwari work motivates its higher, bright, performance-sensitive cluster family.

MIDI Force Input

The CLAP exposes one note input named Gong MIDI Strike In. Note-on is an alternate physical strike: velocity controls contact and nonlinear energy, while the most recent note transposes the shared resonant body around C4 / MIDI note 60. Note-off does not gate or mute the result; the metal body continues its natural decay. CLAP note events and conventional MIDI note-on messages are both accepted, and their frame offsets are honored within each audio block.

MIDI and Gong Force In remain additive, so a performer can strike the model from a keyboard while feeding transducers, contact microphones, envelopes, or audio impulses into the mono force inlet. The procedural exciter remains available as a third concurrent source; set Activity and Ambient to zero for a strictly played instrument.

Model Basis

The core is a compact modal bank: each mode has a frequency, exponential decay, excitation weight, radiation weight, and two orthogonal spatial lobes. Small frequency differences within a pair produce beating, while their changing phase relationship makes the encoded field breathe rather than merely panning a mono gong. In the jing profile, the measured exact harmonics and neighboring components follow different pitch trajectories so impact beats change rate as the softened harmonics recover.

Ayers and Horner’s Gong Ageng resynthesis showed the value of selected harmonic landmarks, differentiated envelopes, strike variation, and synchronized slow/fast AM and FM. Nonlinear gong and plate studies by Bilbao and by Ducceschi, Touzé, and Bilbao identify impact-dependent pitch glide, frequency-dependent loss, and delayed high-frequency energy transfer. Ambi Encoder Modal uses those audible behaviors as bounded real-time trajectories rather than claiming to solve a full nonlinear plate.

Physical observation and encoded direction remain separate. Radiation Center and Spread change where the virtual points hear the body; AED Spread changes only their angular projection. Geometry and weights interpolate over 35 ms so that moving the array remains playable.

See Gong Modal Synthesis and Structural Listening for the research sources.

Presets

The focused bank contains GONG AGENG, SILENT BRONZE, TEMPLE BRONZE, DOUBLE MALLET, MALLET ROLL, BOWED RIM, KKWAENGGWARI LEAD, BRONZE SCRAPE, BRIGHT BOSS, MUTED BLOOM, JING WIND, KKWAENGGWARI CHAE, and JING CEREMONY. Gong Ageng is the default.

CLAP and Renderer Workflow

The bundle is s3g_ambi_encoder_modal.clap and appears in hosts as s3g Ambi Encoder Modal 16. Its native 1160 × 760 interface uses the shared encoder title actions—Preset, Load, Save, and guarded Random—with Output first and separate Exciter, Modal Body, Projection, Radiation Array, and Listener panels.

cmake --preset clap
cmake --build --preset clap --target s3g_accelerometer_field_encoder_clap
./scripts/install-clap-bundles.sh

cmake --build --preset dev --target \
  s3g_accelerometer_field_encoder_smoke \
  s3g_accelerometer_field_encoder_render
./build/s3g_accelerometer_field_encoder_render --list

Limits

The four bodies are compact musical profiles, not calibrated replicas. The engine does not solve full two-dimensional gong geometry, identify one maker’s alloy and hammering pattern, or run the hundreds of modes required for a high-energy nonlinear plate simulation. Coupling and Energy reproduce selected audible consequences within a stable real-time instrument.
