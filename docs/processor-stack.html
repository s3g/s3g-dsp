Processor Stack

s3g Processor Stack is a stereo MIDI instrument in which pickup-viewed plucked strings drive one shared distortion pedal, sagging amplifier, nonlinear loudspeaker, two microphones, and a governed acoustic return. The strings supply scrape, twang, palm damping, and pitch articulation; the amp and speaker feedback circuit still supplies the instrument's weight, squelch, harmonic changes, and unstable sustain.

Version 0.6.1 removes the remaining hard numerical rails from the pedal, cabinet modes, microphones, room history, and output. Speaker stress now shortens modal decay and reduces breakup drive before the sound becomes incoherent; a linear-through-normal-level soft knee remains only as an emergency bound. The microphones share one linked bound, and a finite-attack limiter feeds a continuous soft ceiling. Version 0.6.0's per-string ADSR and dense-arpeggio PICK governor remain intact.

Workflow

  1. Insert s3g Processor Stack on a stereo instrument track and send it MIDI notes.
  2. Monitor the stereo output through speakers or headphones, begin with the CROOKED STACK, BARE STACK, or ONE FINGER RIFF preset, and keep the host track level conservative.
  3. Select POWER for generated root/fifth/octave voicings, HAND for up to four played notes, or LEAD for last-note-priority monophonic lines.
  4. Set PICK, STRING, and DAMP for attack hardness, pickup combing, string decay, and palm-like muting. At low String settings, the pick packet and speaker loop dominate.
  5. Use ATTACK, DECAY, SUSTAIN, and RELEASE to contour each generated string before it enters the pedal. RELEASE closes source energy; SPILL independently controls how long the existing amp and feedback state is inherited.
  6. Leave PATTERN at OFF for direct playing, select a built-in traversal rule, or choose CUSTOM and declare one to eight signed scale degrees with LENGTH and STEP 1–8. Try DECLARED RIFF as a starting point.
  7. Choose a pedal CIRCUIT, then use BITE, STACK, SAG, and CONE to establish the distortion trajectory.
  8. Raise FEEDBACK deliberately. Use PROXIMITY, HARMONIC, TRACK, and signed POLARITY to decide how the microphone return locks. Raise PIERCE and SELF LISTEN when low speaker modes are masking the upper stab; FEEDBACK LANCE demonstrates this regime.
  9. Raise TARGET GLITCH to capture those recognized stabs into note-period micro-cells, then use RATCHET for shorter cells and more repetitions. TARGET ACQUIRED demonstrates the full behavior.
  10. Use OVERLOAD MASK to decide how firmly the circuit clears dense, rough energy. It acts inside the speaker return rather than merely turning down the final output.
  11. Use CROOKED for interval-dependent hesitation, overshoot, repeated-note choke, and squawk. With the arpeggiator off it follows played intervals; with it on, every generated step provokes the same deterministic circuit response.
  12. For tight metal rhythm, start from CHUG CHUG CHUG: its CUSTOM pattern is three root degrees, POWER supplies the fifth, and short gates plus high damping create repeated palm-muted attacks.

Signal Flow

MIDI -> SCALE / ARP RULE -> PICK + PLUCKED STRING / PICKUP -> SHARED PEDAL
     -> PREAMP / TONE -> PUSH-PULL POWER + SHARED SAG
     -> NONLINEAR FOUR-MODE SPEAKER -> MIC A / MIC B + ROOM -> OUTPUT
                                      |
                                      +-> BODY / STAB TARGET DELAYS
                                          -> PERIOD-LOCKED GLITCH CELL
                                          -> DC BLOCK / GOVERNORS
                                          -> PEDAL INPUT
  1. The optional tempo arpeggiator maps the latest held key through a scale and movement rule; POWER, HAND, or LEAD allocation then populates as many as four plucked-string lanes.
  2. The lanes sum before one stateful pedal, preamp, tone path, push-pull power transfer, and shared supply-sag envelope.
  3. The power signal excites four lossy speaker modes plus displacement-dependent cone limiting and breakup.
  4. Two microphone views form the stereo output; their selected combination enters a smoothed, DC-blocked, filtered, and governed delay return.
  5. The return rejoins the exciter sum before the pedal, while linked peak containment operates only at the final output.

The feedback tap follows speaker distortion. Speaker displacement, breakup, voice-coil compression, cabinet modes, and microphone phase therefore change the regenerated signal itself. Moving the return ahead of the speaker would reduce the design to a distortion pedal followed by a resonant delay and is intentionally not offered.

The pedal, amplifier, sag envelope, and main speaker displacement are shared and centered. Stereo begins with two meaningful microphone views: a stable central tap and an off-axis tap mixed with a short room reflection. Difference energy is restrained at low frequencies so power-chord roots remain useful in mono.

MIDI and Performance

ModeBehavior
POWEROne MIDI note populates root, fifth, and octave exciters. Shape moves from root/fifth toward a denser and more inverted voicing.
HANDUp to four played notes enter the same stack. Voice stealing damps the oldest available exciter without resetting pedal, amp, speaker, or feedback state.
LEADMonophonic last-note priority with held-note restoration and legato retuning of the existing circuit.

CROOKED responds to played intervals. Repeated notes choke and re-strike the speaker; seconds and tritones disturb the preferred loop harmonic; fourths, fifths, and octaves acquire it more directly; wide leaps overshoot before settling; and descending legato motion retains the previous attraction longer than ascending motion. The response is deterministic from the note sequence and velocity.

The arpeggiator is deterministic. UP, DOWN, and PENDULUM traverse the selected scale; PEDAL alternates the root with a rising scale position; SCRAMBLE uses a fixed irregular stride. CUSTOM reads STEP 1–8 up to LENGTH. These are signed scale degrees, not semitones: zero is the played root, positive values ascend through the chosen rule, and negative values address degrees below it. Scale rules are CHROMATIC, PHRYGIAN, HARM MIN, DIMINISHED, and TRITONE. Rates include 1/8 through 1/64 with triplets plus 1/4, 1/2, and 1/1 for slow processional riffs, using host tempo when supplied and 120 BPM otherwise. OCTAVES applies to built-in traversals and GATE drives each generated string's ADSR. At dense rates, excess high-PICK energy is normalized from measured recovery and tempo spacing; alternating pick polarity waits until the preceding packet has cleared.

Pick attacks now use a PICK-dependent slew and bandwidth: zero PICK has a rounded, noise-free seed, while higher settings progressively restore a band-pass scrape and fast edge. The maximum setting is deliberately short of full-band noise and uses a slightly slower rise than the previous impulse-like endpoint. Pickup velocity is bounded and band-limited while a string retunes, and repeated-note speaker choking follows a smoothed target. Controls edited during silence are adopted before the next note instead of waking from stale PLAY values; bias-bearing pedal models are also referenced against their idle response. These transitions preserve hard articulation without startup discontinuities that read as digital clicks.

In POWER mode, SHAPE controls sustained chord weighting rather than launching another full-strength exciter each time the octave enters. The root attack remains invariant; fifth and octave pick contributions are energy-budgeted and band-limited separately from their held gains, then bloom into the shared amp. Automating SHAPE crossfades voicing and does not create a new pick packet.

The ADSR belongs to each exciter lane rather than the stereo output. POWER tones share a gesture, HAND notes remain independently articulated, and LEAD or arpeggiator retriggers rise from the current envelope value without a discontinuous reset. Low SUSTAIN and short DECAY/RELEASE create palm-muted chugs; long ATTACK and RELEASE create cabinet swells. Closing the envelope does not erase pedal, sag, speaker, room, or microphone-loop memory.

Pitch bend moves the exciters before the microphone loop completely catches up, so bends pull against feedback. CLAP pressure, MIDI channel pressure, and polyphonic aftertouch increase speaker stress and microphone return. MIDI RECEIVE selects OMNI or channels 1–16.

CROOKED and CHAOS have separate jobs. Crooked changes how performed intervals retune and strike the system. Chaos increases competition among speaker modes and microphone paths; it does not randomize pitch or compose phrases.

Feedback and Safety

The microphone return uses fractional delay interpolation, smoothed delay retuning, DC blocking, nonlinear storage, and two bounded lanes. BODY is a broad high-passed speaker return. STAB uses a shorter note-related delay and a topology-preserving state-variable band around a selected upper partial. Its integrator states, STAB sum, and final return approach their limits continuously rather than striking hard clamps. Independent energy followers and governors keep either lane from monopolizing the loop. SELF LISTEN compares their measured spectral share with the target established by PIERCE; when the upper lane is masked it attenuates BODY and raises STAB inside their separate limits. A final whole-loop governor remains after this spectral negotiation.

TARGET GLITCH does not sample the final output indiscriminately. A note or arpeggiator attack arms the detector, which waits for measurable narrow-band STAB energy before capturing recent upper-return history. The cell spans an integer-like group of target-partial periods; RATCHET shortens that group and raises its finite repeat count. Raised-cosine playback windows remove cell-edge discontinuities. Stronger settings introduce deterministic octave-speed and reverse repetitions, while a dedicated glitch-energy governor keeps the recurrence subordinate to the complete feedback loop. In HAND mode the cell comes from the combined speaker response of all active strings.

OVERLOAD MASK watches smoothed speaker level, voice-coil stress, loop-return energy, and normalized sample-to-sample roughness. A loud but coherent low drone therefore does not receive the same treatment as a dense broadband feedback collapse. When the combined detector rises, a slow mask removes upper speaker energy, reduces STAB and glitch reinjection more strongly than BODY, and lowers requested feedback. Attack and recovery are smoothed, so containment should sound like the cabinet clearing space rather than a digital gate or emergency mute.

SPILL lengthens the held-note gate's release so a new note can inherit the previous speaker state. Measured loop activity cannot reopen that gate. After the final note, the requested return must fall below unity and drain to numerical silence; version 0.5 has no indefinite hold mode.

A linked stereo ceiling catches pathological current-sample peaks. It is a final safeguard rather than the source of feedback regulation. The circuit remains active internally while an audible tail exists and reports a parameter-dependent CLAP tail to the host.

Factory Presets and State

The twenty-two presets are Crooked Stack, Bare Stack, One Finger Riff, Janky Jab, Speaker Cough, Pinched Wire, Brownout Fifth, Room Fight, Welded Chord, Howl on Pressure, Amp Left On, Broken Combo, Phrygian Barrage, Diminished Rake, Pedal Tone Panic, Tritone Scramble, Declared Riff, Feedback Lance, Target Acquired, Chug Chug Chug, Poly Shrapnel, and Slow Monolith. The fast arpeggiator presets use short low-sustain contours, Chug Chug Chug has a near-immediate palm-muted envelope, and Slow Monolith uses a 380 ms attack with a multi-second release for spacious doom movement. Presets use only the public parameter surface; MIDI Receive is left unchanged.

Version 0.6 stores 50 CLAP parameters. Version 0.5's 46-parameter, version 0.4's 45-parameter, version 0.3's 43-parameter, version 0.2's 32-parameter, and version 0.1's 27-parameter states load with safe defaults for later controls. Circuit, voicing, and arpeggiator selections are stepped; continuous controls are smoothed. Circuit changes crossfade stateful transfers over 20 ms. Delay and capture storage is allocated during prepare, with no allocation, locking, file access, or unbounded iteration in audio processing.

Implementation Notes

Four exciter lanes provide a recovery-aware, velocity-shaped pick packet into a fractional-delay string with damping, slight dispersion, nonlinear bridge loss, and a position-dependent pickup comb. Pick direction alternates only after the preceding packet has cleared. Each lane has its own ADSR before their sum enters one pedal and amplifier. A weak transient pitch witness helps the attack, but held tone and decay come from the recirculating string rather than a sustained oscillator bank. The power stage is evaluated at 2x rate around its nonlinear path. Four lossy speaker modes cover low resonance through breakup; displacement moves the lowest mode slightly, while cone limiting and a shared voice-coil envelope provide dynamic compression.

The eight pedal families reuse the repository's clean-room analog-drive transfers. The return's delay/governor grammar is derived from the bounded feedback work in Processor LF Synth, but its protected low crossover and two independent loops are not reused. Processor Stack instead puts the shared nonlinear speaker and microphone signal inside one central feedback system.

The complete design rationale, product boundary, prototype order, and listening tests remain in Processor Stack design and implementation notes.

Limits

Processor Stack is a reduced-order musical circuit, not a component-exact model of a named pedal, amplifier, driver, cabinet, microphone, room, guitar, or guitarist. Its string branch is a compact waveguide rather than a six-string physical reconstruction, and its cabinet modes are not an impulse response. The instrument draws on general power-chord, angular-interval, extreme-metal scalar, feedback-riding, and damaged-amplifier practices without containing recorded material, transcribed phrases, or a replica of a particular performer's tone.
