Ambi Cryosphere Encoder

s3g Ambi Encoder Cryosphere is the frozen counterpart to Pyrosphere: a zero-input geological instrument in which frost and moving ice act on bedrock, substrate, snowpack, and water. Ice growth, strain, fracture, basal slip, calving, impact, cavitation, and granular transport are distributed across as many as 64 moving ACN/SN3D field voices.

Cryogeology design

The source model is aperiodic. Each field voice accumulates frost and ice-growth stress, releases branching fracture cascades, and loads an independent basal stick-slip process. Surface ice additionally behaves as a three-generation plate: broad slab flex, secondary fault lines, and fine radial cracks accumulate pressure independently. Irregular weight steps open bright cracks, which can cascade into a through-rupture, local plate collapse, and a separate low impact. Granular snow and ice transport scrape across the selected substrate. Calving events detach mass first, then schedule a physically separate water impact and cavitation tail. A diffuse filtered body response preserves scale without the persistent inharmonic oscillator bank used by the earlier prototype.

ICE GROWTH, EVENT RATE, SLAB SCALE, BRANCHING, FRACTURE DENSITY, FRACTURE MASS, FRACTURE TAIL, BODY SCATTER, and BRITTLENESS shape solid failure. EVENT RATE is a logarithmic master geological clock from HOLD to 1×; it paces frost strain, basal slip, granular contacts, calving checks, and structural plate loading together, while each event retains its full material articulation. SLAB SCALE, DEPTH, substrate mass, and FRACTURE MASS now span a wider physical body range: large/deep configurations move energy into broad sub-ice pressure and plate-flexure bands, while small/shallow settings remain crisp. Footsteps, through-fracture, calving, and delayed water impacts launch short mass-dependent flexural/ground impulses; these decay with the event and do not create a continuous resonant bed. SURFACE LOAD controls pressure/footstep drive, ICE SNAP exposes fine high-frequency radial failure, and PLATE FAILURE governs through-fracture and local collapse. GRANULES, FROZEN IMPACTS, CALVING, and BRINE POCKETS govern loose material and phase-boundary events. Process profiles distinguish frost cracking, ice segregation, permafrost heave, basal stick-slip, pressure ridging, calving, iceberg impact, avalanche release, snowpack creep, hail, sleet, freezing rain, meltwater under ice, and singing lake ice; substrate profiles distinguish open ice, rock, snowpack, moraine, concrete, metal, glass, ice tunnel, ice cave, and glacier.

SINGING LAKE is the deliberate exception to the otherwise non-pitched body model. A fracture or surface-load impulse launches one dominant floating-plate packet: a dull strike opens into a compact sonar- or laser-like downward chirp, then a quiet inharmonic after-ring decays. SLAB SCALE and DEPTH lower the terminal plate frequency, BRIGHTNESS raises the chirp’s starting point, BODY SCATTER lengthens it, and DAMPING suppresses it. It remains event-triggered and is never left running as a generic resonator in the other regimes.

The fracture, ice-segregation, basal-slip, and iceberg-impact paths are informed by field and laboratory geophysics. In particular, the delayed calving impact/cavitation stage is separate from detachment, while basal slip is treated as load-and-release rather than a continuous tone. This remains a realtime procedural interpretation rather than a predictive glacier model. See References.

Aleatoric entity score

Each field voice now represents a finite plate, loaded substrate region, brine pocket, or detachable ice mass rather than a permanently sounding ambience lane. A deterministic aleatoric score moves these entities through stillness, load or thermal-strain accumulation, an initiating crack, spatial propagation, rupture or displacement, and a settling aftermath. Plates retain integrity and crack extent; brine pressure grows and drains; crack arrivals travel to nearby plates after distance-dependent delays. In calving processes the detached mass becomes a delayed impact, while Ice Under Foot uses the same propagation system as a moving load across successive plates.

PACE moves from long cryogeological arcs to rapid event turnover. OCCUPANCY sets the target number of simultaneously loaded entities. CASCADE governs spatial crack and load transfer, MEMORY controls persistence between macro-scenes, and REST lengthens stillness while removing the residual ice bed. EVENT RATE remains the physical master clock and HOLD still prevents scored directives from producing events.

Presets and SURF

Fourteen factory scenes establish distinct process regions: Alpine Frost Crack, Ice Segregation Gneiss, Permafrost Heave, Basal Glacier Stick-Slip, Pressure Ridge, Calving Front, Iceberg Water Impact, Avalanche Release, Snowpack Creep, Hail on Metal, Sleet on Glass, Meltwater Under Ice, Ice Under Foot, and Singing Lake Ice. The last scene foregrounds isolated dull strikes, compact descending flexural pews, and short lake-plate after-rings.

The SURF page stores complete presets as editable cells. Automatable Surface X/Surface Y coordinates blend continuous event, body, motion, environment, and score parameters; discrete regimes and materials select the nearest cell. VOICES stays an integer readout while surplus frozen-water parcels fade continuously in both synthesis and the field drawing. Curve, focus, and up-to-two-second glide settings are saved with project state; POP opens the synchronized surface in an attached utility panel.

Routing

The 64-channel output exposes first- through seventh-order Ambisonics. Set ORDER, then follow the encoder with the appropriate decoder or field processor.
