Processor No Input Mixer

s3g Processor No Input Mixer 8ch is a zero-audio-input, fixed eight-output CLAP feedback instrument. Each lane has its own resonant body, formant path, four-band EQ, three serial nonlinear inserts, two aux sends, return memory, level, and meter. A signed 8-by-8 matrix and two effect-bearing aux-return loops couple the lanes into one playable electronic ecology.

The instrument combines no-input mixing, configurable feedback circuits, resonant-object excitation, and s3g Matrix movement. Its controls describe musical feedback behaviors rather than recreating a specific circuit or artist's instrument.

Workflow

  1. Set a REAPER track to eight channels, then insert s3g Processor No Input Mixer 8ch. No audio pins or MIDI source are required.
  2. Choose one of the title-band factory presets, or click RANDOM and choose HIGH / QUICK, MID / MODERATE, or LOW / SLOW for a bounded complete-network variation.
  3. Activation injects a deterministic startup packet into the otherwise oscillator-free network. Click NEW to reseed the current patch without changing its matrix or processors.
  4. Begin on PATCH. Drag a source port to a destination port to create a route; repeat the gesture or click an already selected cable to dissolve it. Hold Option while connecting for negative polarity. In GRID, click once to patch or select an intersection and click the selected intersection again to dissolve it.
  5. Add a few off-diagonal routes. Positive gains reinforce a destination; negative gains can inhibit, cancel, or frequency-pull it. Movement can animate only the intersections that remain patched.
  6. Use CLEAR ALL in either patch view to dissolve all sixty-four connections. With the plugin editor focused, Left/Right moves between logical pages and any detached page windows.
  7. Balance FDBK, COUPL, and PHASE before opening insert gain. These controls determine whether the network settles, beats, bursts, or moves toward its governor.
  8. Select MIXER for continuous click-and-drag control of all eight strips, both aux returns, and master tone. Click any lane-slot or aux processor name to choose from the complete effect menu. POP detaches that exact page without duplicating or relocating controls.
  9. Use CHANNEL to select a lane, set BODY, LOSS, TUNE, and FINE, then shape it with EQ and the three insert slots. LOCK quantizes the resonant body around that lane's MIDI-note target.
  10. Use AUX to choose where each lane feeds Aux A/B and how each signed return re-enters each destination. Use SURF to capture and perform between complete network states.
  11. Use SAFETY for output, containment, and PANIC.

RANDOM is an energy-profile menu rather than one uncorrelated roll. Each choice generates a new deterministic seed and varies the complete matrix, bodies, EQ, tuning and lock states, processor types, insert settings, aux taps and signed destination returns, movement field, optional host sync, listening response, articulation behavior, phase, drift, formant, feedback, and coupling. The profile coordinates those dimensions so slow clocks, sparse choke, and deep listening attenuation do not accidentally accumulate into an inaudible start.

LOW / SLOWSparse lower-energy coupling with output compensation, continuously moving Flow/Chase/Swirl fields, approximately 0.03–1 Hz motion, Glide or gently choked Step behavior, shallow REACT, and a still-audible startup packet.

Every profile keeps controller HOLD off, excludes static HOLD as its generated field shape, begins each lane with an immediate processor type, retains diagonal regeneration, leaves the final limiter enabled at -1 dB, and stays inside the DSP's bounded ranges. It does not alter or erase captured SURF cells. NEW changes only the excitation seed and now uses a stronger restart packet. FORGET is a smaller structural mutation. It reconsiders two to ten off-diagonal crosspoints—the number rises with AGENCY. An existing route may disappear or return with reduced gain and reversed polarity; an empty cell may acquire a quiet new route. One aux send and the movement phase also change. Lane bodies, EQ, inserts, master feedback, output, articulation behavior, and safety settings remain intact, so FORGET disrupts the current solution without replacing the instrument.

Feedback Matrix

The default WIRES view places eight source ports at left and eight destination ports at right. Every route is bipolar from -1.00 to +1.00: orange cables are positive and cyan cables are negative. Positive sends the source return into the destination with its waveform polarity unchanged; negative multiplies it by -1 before the destination sum. Negative therefore means inverted feedback, not simply less feedback. It may cancel or inhibit correlated energy, but phase paths, delays, EQ, and nonlinear inserts can make it excite a different resonance or feedback state instead. The magnitude still sets how much signal is coupled. Drag between ports to connect, repeat to dissolve, or click a cable to select it for exact editing in CROSSPOINT; its POSITIVE/NEGATIVE button reverses the selected route without changing its magnitude. The alternate GRID view reads sources across columns and destinations down rows. Clicking an empty intersection patches and selects it; clicking a different existing intersection selects it without changing its gain; clicking the selected intersection again dissolves it. Option-click creates negative polarity. CLEAR ALL dissolves the complete graph from either view. The signal takes at least one sample of return memory before re-entering the matrix, so the graph never forms an algebraic loop.

PHASE blends direct returns with short all-pass-bearing paths. DRIFT introduces slow component-like tolerance movement, and FORMANT blends in a normalized product of low- and high-passed body signals. These are part of the feedback behavior rather than post-effects.

Matrix Movement

MATRIX MOVEMENT separates spatial allocation from articulation. The FIELD bank offers FLOW, PULSE, CHASE, SWIRL, SCAT, and HOLD. FLOW, SPREAD, VORTEX, DEPTH, RATE, and PHASE determine how the field scales each patched crosspoint. GLIDE applies the field continuously. STEP samples and holds it; CUT opens and closes binary route sets; BURST produces irregular clustered ratchets and gaps; and SCRAMBLE rapidly chooses different subsets of the patched graph. These modes affect only existing connections and cannot reverse a route's sign or exceed its stored gain.

The compact CUT bank exposes BEHAV, 0.25–80 Hz EVENT rate, 0.5–250 ms LENGTH, DENSITY, CHAOS, 0.1–20 ms click-safe SLEW, and CHOKE. CHOKE applies the articulated gate after each lane's EQ and three inserts, before its signal returns to the graph and audition output. Use it when saturated feedback refills route-level gaps and makes cuts less distinct. At zero DEPTH, stored gains pass unchanged; at full depth the field uses a perceptual 30 dB contrast law, with binary modes additionally closing their gated routes. Destination normalization follows the unmodulated graph, preventing background gain from masking the motion.

The REACT bank derives another route-gain layer from measured lane activity, analogous to listening from inside the feedback field. FOLLOW follows source energy, AVOID recedes from active destinations, EDGE responds to threshold crossings, and BALANCE compares source and destination activity. DEPTH reaches a 30 dB range, while THRESH, ATTACK, RELEASE, and bipolar POLARITY shape the response. REACT multiplies only patched gains: it cannot invent a connection, reverse its stored sign, or exceed its ceiling.

HOLD freezes field, event, drift, and REACT controller state while audio continues through the held ecology. SLOW extends the field down to one cycle per ten minutes and the event clock down to the same lower bound. SYNC replaces free rate with host-tempo divisions from 1/64 through 8 BARS, with separate FIELD and EVENT divisions.

In WIRES, each bright trace is a 24 kHz scope of the route's measured post-phase, post-gain, post-motion audio contribution, drawn along its cable. Trace shape follows the routed waveform, and visibility follows measured level. The selected route reports dBFS below the wiring. In GRID, crosspoint brightness and the lower gain-modulation groups show the live route gates, align with their source columns, and sit above eight −60-to-0 dBFS output meters.

Mixer and Aux Returns

The MIXER page keeps all eight channel strips, both aux returns, and INTERNAL/HOUSE tone visible together. BODY, LOSS, signed local loop gain, low/mid/high EQ, Aux A/B sends, and the output fader all use smooth click-and-drag tracks. Click a processor name in any lane slot or aux return to open its effect menu. The adjacent EDIT action opens the floating Effect Editor for the selected processor. Use the explicit menu to change processor type; MUTE acts immediately.

POP opens the current page in a synchronized, host-attached window. The detached page keeps the same controls, values, and gestures as its docked view. PATCH, MIXER, CHANNEL, SAFETY, AUX, and SURF can be detached independently; click DOCK to return a page to the main editor.

Aux A and B are genuine internal return loops. Every lane can send independently; each bus has its own dropdown-selected processor from the complete twenty-three-type library, drive, tone, bias, return level, and bounded self-feedback. The dedicated AUX page makes the topology explicit. Each lane and bus selects RETURN, PRE EQ, POST EQ, or POST INSERT as its send tap, then uses a signed -1…+1 destination return. Negative return values invert the processed bus before it excites that lane. The default RETURN taps and alternating Aux B signs preserve the original patch behavior.

Each bus also has a global MUTE ALL button. It applies a clickless four-millisecond gate to that processed aux return everywhere in the eight-lane graph, including cross-aux modulation, without erasing any lane's send level. Unmuting therefore restores the preserved aux mix. INTERNAL tone sits inside the feedback ecology, while HOUSE tone affects only the eight audition outputs. This separation allows bright internal energy to sustain a circuit without forcing the same spectrum into the listening system.

Lanes and EQ

The CHANNEL page keeps all eight lane summaries visible while the selected lane's exact body, tuning, EQ, and insert controls occupy the same page. Each of its three insert rows repeats the Mixer's explicit type menu plus EDIT action, so the shared Effect Editor can be opened from either the docked or floating Channel page. BODY moves a four-resonator bank through a different deterministic material variation on every lane. LOSS controls its decay. TUNE is a continuous MIDI-note target, FINE adds ±100 cents, and LOCK suppresses body-frequency drift around that target; using different notes across the eight lanes creates a tuned polyphonic feedback instrument. LEVEL affects only the lane's output audition, while MUTE removes it from the output and allows its internal ecology to continue. KILL clears that lane's body, filters, nonlinear memories, and returns.

The lane EQ contains low shelf, swept mid frequency and gain, and high shelf controls. It sits inside the loop before the inserts, so boosts and cuts alter both timbre and feedback stability.

Each lane owns three independently selectable serial slots. Type changes use a short crossfade, and processing can run at 1x, 2x, or 4x quality. The library covers distortion, switching, buffer, modulation, voice transformation, and octave-generation families. SURF Parameter Surface

SURF brings the shared s3g Parameter Surface to the feedback network. ADD captures the current base patch as a cell; CAP replaces the selected cell; DEL removes it. After at least two cells exist, switch the surface ON and use PLAY to drag the crosshair. FOCUS shapes spatial influence, CURVE selects the interpolation response, and GLIDE gives the active cursor a click-safe settling time. Automatable, modulatable SURFACE X and SURFACE Y parameters make the same gesture available to the host.

Continuous network, matrix, processor, tuning, movement, REACT, and aux-topology values blend by distance. Processor choices, tap locations, movement shapes, clock modes, and other discrete topology follow the nearest cell. Final output gain, ceiling, limiter/DC settings, quality, lane faders/mutes, aux global mutes, house tone, and preset variance remain live outside the surface, so containment and audition controls never disappear under an interpolated scene. A whole-patch preset, RANDOM, or FORGET bypasses SURF without deleting its cells.

Project state stores cell parameter snapshots but never feedback delay, resonator, splice-buffer, envelope, or other live audio memory. Moving between cells therefore steers one continuing electronic ecology instead of crossfading recordings of earlier sound.

Containment

Per-lane RMS and peak observers drive a slow feedback governor only when loop energy becomes unsafe. Normal transients are not treated as compressor input. Optional DC blocking surrounds the feedback path, and eight independent ceiling limiters protect the final outputs. The monitor reports QUIET, STABLE, EDGE, or RUNAWAY.

PANIC ramps the outputs down, clears all feedback, resonator, filter, oversampling, and limiter memories, and rearms from silence. It does not automatically reseed; click NEW when a fresh excitation is wanted.

Automation and State

The plugin exposes 400 CLAP parameters for host automation: 56 globals; all sixty-four signed matrix gains; seventeen direct controls per lane, including tuning and aux topology; and six controls per insert slot. Continuous controls also advertise CLAP modulation, and parameter/modulation events take effect at their sample offsets inside each process block. Project state stores the full patch, SURF cells and interpolation settings, selected lane/slot/source/destination, aux mutes, behavior state, and editor page. Versions 1–4 migrate into conservative defaults for controls that did not yet exist; saving again writes state version 5.
