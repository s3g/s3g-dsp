Processor No Input Mixer

s3g Processor No Input Mixer 8ch is a zero-audio-input, fixed eight-output CLAP feedback instrument. Each lane has its own resonant body, formant path, four-band EQ, three serial nonlinear inserts, two aux sends, return memory, level, and meter. A signed 8-by-8 matrix and two effect-bearing aux-return loops couple the lanes into one playable electronic ecology.

The instrument hybridizes no-input mixing, configurable feedback circuits, resonant-object excitation, and the existing s3g Matrix movement engine. It is a new clean-room DSP design, not a circuit reconstruction, artist emulation, or transcription of a historical instrument.

Workflow

  1. Set a REAPER track to eight channels, then insert s3g Processor No Input Mixer 8ch. No audio pins or MIDI source are required.
  2. Choose one of the title-band factory presets, or click RANDOM for a bounded complete-network variation.
  3. Activation injects a deterministic startup packet into the otherwise oscillator-free network. Click NEW to reseed the current patch without changing its matrix or processors.
  4. Begin on PATCH. Drag a source port to a destination port to create a route; repeat the gesture or click an already selected cable to dissolve it. Hold Option while connecting for negative polarity. In GRID, click once to patch or select an intersection and click the selected intersection again to dissolve it.
  5. Add a few off-diagonal routes. Positive gains reinforce a destination; negative gains can inhibit, cancel, or frequency-pull it. Movement can animate only the intersections that remain patched.
  6. Use CLEAR ALL in either patch view to dissolve all sixty-four connections. With the plugin editor focused, Left/Right moves between logical pages and any detached page windows.
  7. Balance FDBK, COUPL, and PHASE before opening insert gain. These controls determine whether the network settles, beats, bursts, or moves toward its governor.
  8. Select MIXER for continuous click-and-drag control of all eight strips, both aux returns, and master tone. Click any lane-slot or aux processor name to choose from the complete effect menu. POP detaches that exact page without duplicating or relocating controls.
  9. Use CHANNEL to select a lane, tune BODY and LOSS, then shape it with EQ and the three insert slots.
  10. Use SAFETY for output and containment. PANIC remains fixed in the title band on every page and detached window.

Presets and Randomization

The title-band PRESET menu contains ten complete starting states. Selecting one replaces the global network, all sixty-four matrix gains, eight bodies and EQs, twenty-four insert slots, aux system, and movement state, then applies the current VARIANCE and injects a deterministic seed.

RANDOM generates a new deterministic seed and varies the complete matrix, bodies, EQ, processor types, insert settings, aux returns, movement field, articulation behavior, phase, drift, formant, feedback, and coupling. It keeps the final limiter enabled, the ceiling at -1 dB, output trim conservative, diagonal regeneration present on every lane, and all generated values inside the DSP's bounded operating ranges. NEW changes only the excitation seed. FORGET is a smaller structural mutation. It reconsiders two to ten off-diagonal crosspoints—the number rises with AGENCY. An existing route may disappear or return with reduced gain and reversed polarity; an empty cell may acquire a quiet new route. One aux send and the movement phase also change. Lane bodies, EQ, inserts, master feedback, output, articulation behavior, and safety settings remain intact, so FORGET disrupts the current solution without replacing the instrument.

Feedback Matrix

The default WIRES view places eight source ports at left and eight destination ports at right. Every route is bipolar from -1.00 to +1.00: orange cables are positive and cyan cables are negative. Positive sends the source return into the destination with its waveform polarity unchanged; negative multiplies it by -1 before the destination sum. Negative therefore means inverted feedback, not simply less feedback. It may cancel or inhibit correlated energy, but phase paths, delays, EQ, and nonlinear inserts can make it excite a different resonance or feedback state instead. The magnitude still sets how much signal is coupled. Drag between ports to connect, repeat to dissolve, or click a cable to select it for exact editing in CROSSPOINT; its POSITIVE/NEGATIVE button reverses the selected route without changing its magnitude. The alternate GRID view reads sources across columns and destinations down rows. Clicking an empty intersection patches and selects it; clicking a different existing intersection selects it without changing its gain; clicking the selected intersection again dissolves it. Option-click creates negative polarity. CLEAR ALL dissolves the complete graph from either view. The signal takes at least one sample of return memory before re-entering the matrix, so the graph never forms an algebraic loop.

PHASE blends direct returns with short all-pass-bearing paths. DRIFT introduces slow component-like tolerance movement, and FORMANT blends in a normalized product of low- and high-passed body signals. These are part of the feedback behavior rather than post-effects.

Matrix Movement

MATRIX MOVEMENT separates spatial allocation from articulation. The FIELD bank retains the s3g Matrix vocabulary: FLOW, PULSE, CHASE, SWIRL, SCAT, and HOLD. FLOW, SPREAD, VORTEX, DEPTH, RATE, and PHASE describe how that field scales each patched crosspoint. GLIDE is the continuous original behavior. STEP samples and holds the field; CUT opens and closes binary route sets; BURST produces irregular clustered ratchets and gaps; and SCRAMBLE rapidly chooses different subsets of the existing graph. None of these modes can invent a connection, reverse its sign, or exceed its stored gain.

The compact CUT bank exposes BEHAV, 0.25–80 Hz EVENT rate, 0.5–250 ms LENGTH, DENSITY, CHAOS, 0.1–20 ms click-safe SLEW, and CHOKE. CHOKE applies the articulated gate after each lane's EQ and three inserts, before its signal returns to the graph and audition output. This is deliberate: saturated feedback can refill a route-level gap, whereas the lane-return choke makes cuts remain audible. At zero DEPTH, stored gains pass unchanged; at full depth the field uses a perceptual 30 dB contrast law, with binary modes additionally closing their gated routes. Destination normalization follows the unmodulated graph and therefore cannot boost the background to conceal the motion.

WIRES does not substitute an abstract movement animation for the result: each bright trace is a 24 kHz scope of the route's measured post-phase, post-gain, post-motion audio contribution, bent around its cable. Trace shape is the real waveform and trace visibility follows measured level. The selected route reports dBFS below the wiring. In GRID, crosspoint brightness and the lower gain-modulation groups read the live DSP route gates, align with their source columns, and sit above eight visible −60-to-0 dBFS output meters.

Mixer and Aux Returns

The MIXER page keeps all eight channel strips, both aux returns, and INTERNAL/HOUSE tone visible together. BODY, LOSS, signed local loop gain, low/mid/high EQ, Aux A/B sends, and the output fader all use smooth click-and-drag tracks. Clicking a processor name in any of the three lane slots selects that slot and opens the complete effect menu; clicking either aux processor name opens the same menu for that return. The adjacent EDIT action opens one shared floating Effect Editor for the selected lane slot or aux return. There is no hidden click-to-cycle behavior. MUTE remains an immediate action.

POP opens the current logical page in a synchronized, host-attached window without creating a second DSP instance. The detached MIXER is identical to the nested page: it uses the same renderer, positions, values, and gestures, so no controls migrate or appear only after detaching. PATCH, CHANNEL, and SAFETY can detach independently as well; DOCK closes a detached page back into the editor.

Aux A and B are genuine internal return loops. Every lane can send independently; each bus has its own dropdown-selected processor from the complete twenty-three-type library, drive, tone, bias, return level, and bounded self-feedback. Each bus also has a global MUTE ALL button. It applies a clickless four-millisecond gate to that processed aux return everywhere in the eight-lane graph, including cross-aux modulation, without erasing any lane's send level. Unmuting therefore restores the preserved aux mix. INTERNAL tone sits inside the feedback ecology, while HOUSE tone affects only the eight audition outputs. This separation allows bright internal energy to sustain a circuit without forcing the same spectrum into the listening system.

Lanes and EQ

The CHANNEL page keeps all eight lane summaries visible while the selected lane's exact body, EQ, and insert controls occupy the same page. Each of its three insert rows repeats the Mixer's explicit type menu plus EDIT action, so the shared Effect Editor can be opened from either the docked or floating Channel page. BODY moves a four-resonator bank through a different deterministic material variation on every lane. LOSS controls its decay. LEVEL affects only the lane's output audition, while MUTE removes it from the output and allows its internal ecology to continue. KILL clears that lane's body, filters, nonlinear memories, and returns.

The lane EQ contains low shelf, swept mid frequency and gain, and high shelf controls. It sits inside the loop before the inserts, so boosts and cuts alter both timbre and feedback stability.

Processor Inserts

Each lane owns three independently selectable serial slots. Type changes use a short crossfade and processing can run at 1x, 2x, or 4x quality. The library now spans distortion, switching, buffer, modulation, voice transformation, and octave-generation families. BYPASS removes only the selected lane slot. Aux editing substitutes RETURN and LOOP for lane level and bypass.

Containment

Per-lane RMS and peak observers drive a slow feedback governor only when loop energy becomes unsafe. Normal transients are not treated as compressor input. Optional DC blocking surrounds the feedback path, and eight independent ceiling limiters protect the final outputs. The monitor reports QUIET, STABLE, EDGE, or RUNAWAY.

PANIC ramps the outputs down, clears all feedback, resonator, filter, oversampling, and limiter memories, and rearms from silence. It does not automatically reseed; click NEW when a fresh excitation is wanted.

Automation and State

The plugin exposes 331 stable CLAP parameters: forty-three global network, movement, articulation, tone, aux, output, and containment controls; sixty-four signed matrix gains; ten direct controls for each lane; and six controls for each of the twenty-four insert slots. The two added global parameters expose Aux A/B processor bias that was already part of stored DSP state. Factory presets, variance, randomization, forgetting, the articulation layer, and the two global aux mutes resolve into ordinary stored state. The selected lane, slot, source, destination, and editor page are stored as well. Version-one and version-two project state migrate with both aux buses unmuted and GLIDE articulation; version-three state preserves its aux mutes and also initializes GLIDE. Processing allocates no memory after activation.
