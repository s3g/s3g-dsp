Ambi Encoder Membrane Kick

Ambi Encoder Membrane Kick is a deep, pitch-dropping bass-drum synthesizer whose spatial field comes from the vibrating body itself. Twelve shared modes are sampled at sixteen positions on one membrane; those positions radiate from different directions in selectable first-, second-, or third-order ACN/SN3D. The result is one coherent strike that bends and decays across space rather than a mono kick sent through a panner.

This instrument is currently a source-build preview in the Ambi Encoder family. Configure with -DS3G_BUILD_CLAP_PLUGIN=ON -DS3G_BUILD_FUTURE_COMPONENTS=ON to build s3g Ambi Encoder Membrane Kick 16. It is intentionally excluded from the current packaged pre-release manifest.

Workflow

  1. Set the track to 16 channels, insert s3g Ambi Encoder Membrane Kick 16, and choose 1OA, 2OA, or 3OA from ORDER.
  2. Follow it with an ambisonic decoder.
  3. Send MIDI notes, press STRIKE, or click the membrane display. Open the membrane STRIKE page to automate X and Y, or choose a randomized placement mode.
  4. Set TUNE for the settled low frequency, then balance DROP, TIME, and PUNCH for the attack trajectory.
  5. Choose a membrane SHAPE and raise AMOUNT. Shape changes the modal tuning and the distributed surface coordinates together.
  6. Use SPREAD, DEPTH, and ROTATE to place the complete membrane in the ambisonic field.

Signal Flow

  1. A MIDI note or editor strike applies one force at the selected X/Y point. Velocity changes the accepted force according to VELOCITY.
  2. The force excites twelve modal identities. DROP and TIME move their shared frequency scale rapidly toward TUNE; DECAY and DAMP remove energy from the complete body.
  3. Each mode is sampled at sixteen surface patches. Off-center strikes produce asymmetric mode weights, while a central strike emphasizes the fundamental.
  4. Each patch is saturated before encoding. DRIVE therefore changes the local membrane radiation without independently clipping ambisonic channels.
  5. The patches are encoded from their shaped coordinates. A linked final guard applies one gain to the complete frame so the spatial ratios remain intact.

Shape and Strike Position

CIRCLE begins with the familiar split modal pairs of a symmetric membrane. ELLIPSE separates the horizontal and vertical pairs, SQUARE moves toward rectangular mode relationships, TRIANGLE creates threefold geometry, and IRREGULAR removes the remaining degeneracies. AMOUNT interpolates from the circular body toward the selected shape.

The editor draws the active outline, the sixteen radiation patches, and the current strike crosshair. Moving the crosshair changes which modes accept the impact: central positions favor the long fundamental, while edge positions bring more asymmetric overtones and stronger directional change.

The membrane panel has BODY and STRIKE pages. On STRIKE, X and Y are ordinary automatable CLAP parameters. MODE: FIXED follows those coordinates, RANDOM AREA chooses a new position across the usable surface for every hit, and RANDOM RIM chooses a new position in an outer annulus to emphasize asymmetric upper modes. The placement mode is also automatable. The crosshair shows the position actually used by the most recent hit, including randomized hits.

Clicking the membrane is a direct performance gesture: it switches placement to FIXED, writes the chosen X/Y position, and triggers the body.

Deep Bass Controls

TUNE is the frequency reached after the pitch drop. The default 43 Hz body sits in the low-bass range; DROP starts the modes above it and TIME controls how quickly they fall. PUNCH adds force to the fundamental, while CLICK adds a short high-passed impact localized near the strike point. Higher DAMP values shorten upper modes more strongly than the fundamental.

MIDI Behavior

Every note-on strikes the shared membrane; note-off is ignored because the body rings naturally. TRACK defaults to 100%, so MIDI note 36 plays the frequency set by TUNE and every semitone transposes the complete membrane chromatically. Lower TRACK for partial tracking, or set it to 0% when every key should play the same kick pitch.

VELOCITY also defaults to 100%. Note-on velocity scales the modal force, fundamental punch, and localized click together, so soft notes produce genuinely softer membrane motion rather than only turning down the output afterward. Lower VELOCITY to compress the performance dynamics. The instrument is one shared physical body rather than a polyphonic bank, so a new hit adds energy and restarts the global pitch trajectory.

Automotive Low-Frequency Reference

Automotive measurements provide a useful target context without requiring the synthesizer to imitate one particular subwoofer. Rieder reports low-frequency cabin gain over roughly the first three octaves alongside masking from road noise. Benedict and colleagues measured 1–200 Hz response at eight passenger and inter-seat positions, while Nakanishi and Gerges describe the irregular cabin modes and high pressures produced at resonance. These findings support long low fundamentals, controlled pitch-drop energy, and deliberate modal variation, but also show why one baked-in “car EQ” would not transfer reliably between vehicles or seats.

Lee, Cho, and Ih are especially relevant to this instrument: their automotive low-frequency system uses a thin roof panel with sixteen actuators, an approximately elliptical radiating zone, and inverse vibro-acoustic rendering toward an ear position. Membrane Kick similarly treats shape and distributed radiation as part of the source. The plugin therefore keeps its body model and HOA output general, while a downstream cabin renderer can supply the vehicle- and seat-specific transfer path. See Automotive Low-Frequency Audio and Cabin Response.

Listener-Model Boundary

Membrane Kick deliberately has no internal Listener Mode. Its modal body, fixed or randomized strike placement, and pre-output HOA field remain open-loop so enabling or disabling an analysis layer cannot alter the source tone, transient, or CPU path. This keeps the instrument predictable for low-frequency work and makes FIXED placement sample-repeatable.

Vehicle- and seat-dependent listening remains appropriate as a downstream cabin renderer or monitoring model. Frank and colleagues’ seat-dependent magnitude-and-phase measurements and Yang and colleagues’ source/path separation support that boundary: Membrane Kick supplies the distributed source, while another stage supplies the cabin and listener transfer path.

Limits

The shape families are musical modal approximations, not finite-element reconstructions of calibrated drum shells or heads. The instrument does not accept audio input and does not follow host tempo. Its shared-body retriggering is designed for kick and tom gestures rather than independent overlapping pitched voices. See Modal Synthesis and Physical Modeling for related membrane and resonator references.
